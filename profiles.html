02 / DRAW THE PROFILES

Give the loft two ends.

Draw one closed region on the foot and one on the plate. Keep them separated and use four sides on both for your first attempt.

Profile A = startProfile B = endExample sizes, not measured
Choose placement before dimensions.

The rectangles should fit fully on solid mount material, and the volume between them should stay clear of hardware. The 26 × 8 mm and 18 × 6 mm dimensions are practice values, not validated sizes for your mount. Enter units explicitly if your document uses inches.

A / FOOT

Create the base profile

  1. Get a clear view. In Parts, right-click Camera mount → Zoom to selection. Hide the camera composite if it blocks the mount side.
  2. Select a flat top face of the foot. Choose Sketch. The face itself supplies the sketch plane; an offset plane is not required.
  3. Draw a corner rectangle inside the usable foot area. Start with a small footprint away from bolt holes and tool access.
  4. Size and locate it. Use Dimension for width, depth, and distances to stable edges. Keep construction geometry out of the loop.
  5. Finish the sketch with the green check. Rename it Loft A — foot.
B / CAMERA PLATE

Inspect “Camera loft”

  1. Right-click Camera loft → Edit. Its inspected plane is Face of Extrude 3. Use a normal view to see its outline clearly.
  2. Check that it is the intended support-side face. If it is the wrong face, cancel and create a separate sketch on the correct mount face.
  3. Reuse one suitable closed rectangle. If none exists, draw a small rectangle on this face, above and separate from profile A. Do not select the entire plate with its bolt holes.
  4. Dimension the region and its position. Keep it inside the plate and aligned with A along the mount's width. Use matching corner order on both profiles.
  5. Finish the sketch. Keep the name Camera loft or rename it Loft B — plate so you recognize it.
BEFORE YOU OPEN LOFT

Two regions, two planes

Hover inside each loop. You should be able to identify a filled sketch region, not only individual lines. If the mount face obscures the sketch, temporarily hide the Camera mount part while checking the sketch regions.

  • Close any tiny gaps with Coincident constraints.
  • Remove duplicate or overlapping sketch segments.
  • Keep the profiles physically separate; avoid crossing loops.
  • Each profile must contact its mount face over an area, not only touch at a point or edge.

No plane button needed? Correct. Existing flat faces can be sketch planes. Use Plane → Offset only when you need a profile floating away from a face.
